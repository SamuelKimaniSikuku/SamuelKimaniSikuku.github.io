Why Are Kenyans So Good at Running?

2026

Why are Kenyans so good at running?

Yesterday, Kenya did it again. Sabastian Sawe became the first man in history to officially run a marathon in under 2 hours — 1:59:30 at the London Marathon.

Yomif Kejelcha of Ethiopia also went under 2 hours, finishing second in 1:59:41.

Some of you are thinking, “Didn’t Kipchoge already do this in 2019?” Yes and no. That run in Vienna had 41 pacemakers and drinks delivered by bicycle. Many called it a drill, not a marathon.

What Sawe did yesterday was different. Open race. Real competition. Same rules everyone else runs by.

Scientists once said sub-2 would not happen until around 2075. Some said the human body could die trying. It happened in 2026, by a Kenyan.

Kenya has 58 million people. 0.7% of the world. And we keep winning. So why are Kenyans so good?

Let me share my observation. People always say it is the altitude. Kenyans live high up, so their lungs are stronger.

But that is not the answer. Tibet, Bolivia, and parts of Nepal also sit high up and do not produce world champions. If altitude alone made runners, every mountain village on earth would be producing Kipchoges. Altitude helps. But it is the bonus, not the base.

Here is the base. History: the Kalenjin, who produce most of our champions, are Nilotes. Their ancestors moved south from the Nile Valley and lived as pastoralists in the Rift Valley for centuries, walking and running long distances, moving cattle. Their bodies adapted. Long, thin legs. Narrow feet. Low body mass. Most Kalenjin are no longer pastoralists, but the body that history built is still there.

Childhood: most of us ran to school. 5 to 10 km. Every morning. Back home for lunch. Back to school. Home again in the evening. Up to 30 km a day, before the age of 12. No coach. No watch. No Strava. By the time we were teenagers, we had trained for years without knowing it.

School sport: sport is built into the school system from primary school. Schools compete against zones, districts, all the way to nationals. I was running 15 km cross-country in class 5. By 18, a Kenyan athlete has 10+ years of racing in their legs.

Community: if you want to be a great runner in Kenya, you move to where the best train. Iten. Kaptagat. Eldoret. You show up at 5 am. You run with them. For free. Nobody asks for your CV. You just have to keep up. The pace, the discipline, the mindset, you absorb by being there. That is how Kipchoge trained for 20 years.

Scientists said 2075. Kenya said 2026. No human is limited.

So tell me, what does running mean to you?
